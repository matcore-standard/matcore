    2.7 Phase Field (PF) Metadata

    Version 0.9.0

    The metadata for Phase Field (PF) data (MatCore-PF) is tabulated below. This metadata documents key aspects of the PF method to provide understanding of the dataset generated by it. This includes the fundamental mathematical and physical framework governing the PF simulation, including the governing equations and thermodynamic free energy models. The properties are given in settings blocks, each naming the PF computations it describes. (Note: This standard inherits the metadata in the minimal MatCore standard, adding the properties below.)

    * required appears at most once repeatable

    settings*

    Settings shared by the computations named in it.

    Required Repeatable
    yes yes
    computation-label*

    The label of a computation these settings apply to.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes yes string [] inapplicable —
    physical-phenomenon*

    Material process being modeled; description of the overarching processes and mechanisms being modeled/simulated.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes yes string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Isothermal solidification, Directional solidification, Isothermal annealing, Sintering, Twinning, Grain growth, Spinodal decomposition, Fracture, Plasticity. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Isothermal solidification" – Growth of a solid from a melt or solution held at one uniform temperature, e.g. dendrites growing into an undercooled melt
    • "Directional solidification" – Solidification in an imposed temperature gradient, the front advancing along it, e.g. cellular or dendritic growth in a pulled sample
    • "Isothermal annealing" – Evolution of a solid microstructure held at one temperature, e.g. recovery, recrystallization or precipitate coarsening
    • "Sintering" – Bonding and densification of particles by diffusion below their melting point
    • "Twinning" – Formation or growth of twins, domains whose lattice is the mirror image of the parent's across a twin plane
    • "Grain growth" – Coarsening of a polycrystal by grain-boundary migration, driven by the reduction of grain-boundary energy
    • "Spinodal decomposition" – Phase separation of an unstable solution by the growth of composition fluctuations, without a nucleation barrier
    • "Fracture" – Initiation and propagation of cracks, a phase field marking the broken material
    • "Plasticity" – Irreversible deformation, e.g. by dislocation motion, described by phase fields or coupled to the phase field model

    Note: Give one entry for each process modeled.

    problem-specification*

    The fundamental mathematical and physical framework governing the phase field simulation, including the governing equations and thermodynamic free energy models.

    Required Repeatable
    yes no
    governing-equation*

    The specific phase field equation used to evolve a field variable.

    Required Repeatable
    yes yes

    Note: Give one entry for each equation. Equations solved together form a coupled model.

    type*

    The mathematical classification of the phase field equation.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes yes string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: CH, AC, GP, FID, PFC, Navier-Stokes, Multi-phase-field, Mechanical equilibrium, Poisson. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "CH" – Cahn-Hilliard: a conserved field evolves by a flux driven by the gradient of the variational derivative of the free energy
    • "AC" – Allen-Cahn: a nonconserved field relaxes at a rate proportional to the variational derivative of the free energy
    • "GP" – Grand potential: the phase fields evolve with the grand potential, the chemical potentials being the diffusion variables instead of the concentrations
    • "FID" – Finite interface dissipation: each phase has its own concentration within the interface, and the phases exchange solute at a finite rate
    • "PFC" – Phase field crystal: a conserved field periodic on the atomic scale resolves the crystal lattice and its defects
    • "Navier-Stokes" – The flow of a fluid phase; the evolved variable is the velocity
    • "Multi-phase-field" – Several phase fields, one for each phase or grain, constrained to sum to one
    • "Mechanical equilibrium" – The balance of stresses; the evolved variable is the displacement
    • "Poisson" – Electrostatics; the evolved variable is the electric potential

    Note: Give one entry for each classification that applies, e.g. AC and Multi-phase-field. Give an anti-trapping current, or a sharp- or thin-interface formulation, as a user-specified value.

    evolved-variable*

    The name of the field-variable being evolved by this equation.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Examples: phi, c.

    Clarifications: The name of a field variable defined in variables › field-variable of the same settings block.

    free-energy-density-term

    A term of the free energy density that drives this equation.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no yes string [] inapplicable —

    Examples: f_chem, f_elastic.

    Clarifications: The name of a term defined in problem-specification › free-energy-density of the same settings block.

    free-energy-density*

    A term of the thermodynamic free energy density used in the simulation.

    Required Repeatable
    yes yes

    Note: Give one entry for each term, e.g. a chemical, an interfacial and an elastic term.

    name*

    The identifier or symbol used for this specific energy functional.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Examples: f_chem, Chemical Free Energy.

    contribution

    The physical contribution to the free energy that this term represents.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no yes string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Chemical, Interfacial, Elastic, Plastic, Electrical, Magnetic, Fluid-solid interaction, Solid-solid interaction. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Chemical" – The bulk free energy of the phases as a function of composition and temperature
    • "Interfacial" – The energy of interfaces, from the gradient terms and the barrier between phases
    • "Elastic" – The elastic strain energy
    • "Plastic" – The energy stored by plastic deformation
    • "Electrical" – The energy of charges, polarization and electric fields
    • "Magnetic" – The energy of magnetization and magnetic fields
    • "Fluid-solid interaction" – The energy of the coupling between a fluid and a solid
    • "Solid-solid interaction" – The energy of the coupling between solid phases or grains

    Note: A term that combines several contributions gives one entry for each.

    description*

    The type/derivation of the thermodynamic bulk free energy function.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Ideal, Regular, Flory, Calphad, Poly, Non-polynomial. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Ideal" – Ideal solution: the entropy of ideal mixing, with no interaction energy
    • "Regular" – Regular solution: ideal mixing with an interaction energy
    • "Flory" – Flory-Huggins: the free energy of mixing of polymer solutions or blends
    • "Calphad" – Gibbs energies from a CALPHAD thermodynamic database
    • "Poly" – A polynomial in the field variables, e.g. a double well or a Landau expansion
    • "Non-polynomial" – Any other closed-form function of the field variables
    expression

    The mathematical expression of the free energy density.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: A code snippet, LaTeX formula, or SymPy expression.

    unit

    The physical unit of the free energy density.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: A symbol from the MatCore Unit Table (Annex U), or the reserved symbol dimensionless. If and only if no Annex U symbol applies, a string prefixed x-gnu: giving a GNU Units expression (accepted with a validation warning).

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Cartesian, Cylindrical, Spherical. Other values are allowed.

    domain-size

    Specification of the computational region extents.

    Required Repeatable
    no no

    Note: Give one extent for each coordinate direction, in the order of the coordinates.

    Note: unit is a Length symbol from Annex U, or dimensionless when the simulation uses reduced units; the characteristic length is then given as a variables › model-parameter entry, so the extents can be converted.

    Note: For a regular domain the extents are its size. For an irregular geometry, whose mesh is given in numerical-method › mesh-file, they may give a characteristic size of the structure.

    value*

    The value of the property, in the unit given by unit.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no float [:] Length m

    Minimum: 0, for each component.

    unit*

    The unit of the value, an Annex U symbol whose Kind is the Kind of value.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —
    boundary-condition

    A constraint applied on a boundary of the computational domain.

    Required Repeatable
    no yes

    Note: Give one entry for each condition. An entry without boundary applies to every boundary, and one without field-variable to every field variable.

    type*

    The kind of boundary condition.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Dirichlet, Neumann, Zero-Neumann, Periodic. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Dirichlet" – The value of the field variable is prescribed on the boundary, e.g. a fixed concentration or a clamped displacement
    • "Neumann" – The normal derivative of the field variable (its flux) is prescribed on the boundary
    • "Zero-Neumann" – The normal derivative is zero: no flux crosses the boundary, or a surface is free of traction
    • "Periodic" – Opposite boundaries are identified, so the field variable repeats across them
    boundary

    The boundary on which the condition applies.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: The name of a boundary in the mesh file (numerical-method › mesh-file of the same settings block), or a description such as "x = 0".

    field-variable

    The field variable that the condition constrains.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: The name of a field variable defined in variables › field-variable of the same settings block.

    initial-condition

    The spatial distribution of the field variables at the start of the simulation.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no yes string [] inapplicable —

    Examples: Uniform constant, Random noise, Geometric seed, Imported array.

    Clarifications: A mathematical function, or a state produced by an algorithm or given as a spatial distribution, is described in words.

    numerical-method*

    A description of the computational framework used to solve the phase field equations.

    Required Repeatable
    yes yes
    evolved-variable

    The field variable evolved by an equation that this entry solves.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no yes string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: The name of a field variable defined in variables › field-variable of the same settings block, evolved by one of the problem-specification › governing-equation entries.

    Note: Give it when equations are solved by different methods or solvers, one numerical-method entry for each group, each naming the variables its equations evolve. Omit it when one entry covers every equation.

    spatial-discretization*

    The numerical approach used to convert continuous governing equations into a system of algebraic equations defined over a discrete mesh or set of points within the computational domain.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: FDM, FEM, FVM, LBM, Particle, Spectral. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "FDM" – Finite Difference Method: derivatives are approximated by differences of the values at grid points
    • "FEM" – Finite Element Method: the fields are expanded in basis functions on elements, and the equations are imposed in weak form
    • "FVM" – Finite Volume Method: the equations are integrated over control volumes, balancing the fluxes across their faces
    • "LBM" – Lattice Boltzmann Method: distribution functions are streamed and collided on a lattice
    • "Particle" – The fields are carried by moving particles or points, e.g. smoothed particle hydrodynamics or the material point method
    • "Spectral" – The fields are expanded in global basis functions, e.g. Fourier modes evaluated by fast Fourier transforms
    spatial-discretization-parameter

    A parameter of the spatial discretization.

    Required Repeatable
    no yes

    Note: For example, the element degree, the quadrature rule or the finite-difference stencil. The grid spacing is given in grid-spacing.

    name*

    The designation of the spatial discretization parameter.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —
    value*

    The data associated with the spatial discretization parameter.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no any any any —
    unit

    The unit in which the associated value is expressed.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Clarifications: A symbol from the MatCore Unit Table (Annex U), or the reserved symbol dimensionless. If and only if no Annex U symbol applies, a string prefixed x-gnu: giving a GNU Units expression (accepted with a validation warning).

    Note: Give it when the type of the value is float or int; omit it when it is string or bool.

    description

    An explanation of the meaning, source, or purpose of the spatial discretization parameter.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —
    spatial-accuracy-order

    The exponent of the grid spacing parameter (Δx) determining the rate at which the discretization error approaches zero as the mesh is refined.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no int [] dimensionless —

    Minimum: 1.

    mesh-type

    The topology or distribution of the discrete grid points.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Regular, Rectilinear, Skewed, Curvilinear, Unstructured, Adaptive mesh refinement, Meshless. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Regular" – A structured grid with uniform spacing along each axis
    • "Rectilinear" – A structured grid of axis-aligned cells whose spacing varies along an axis
    • "Skewed" – A structured grid whose axes are not orthogonal
    • "Curvilinear" – A structured grid whose lines follow curved coordinates, e.g. fitted to a boundary
    • "Unstructured" – Cells of arbitrary connectivity, e.g. triangles or tetrahedra
    • "Adaptive mesh refinement" – A grid refined and coarsened locally during the simulation, e.g. at interfaces
    • "Meshless" – Points without connectivity, as in particle methods
    mesh-file

    A file holding the mesh or part of it, such as its structure or the boundaries on which the boundary conditions apply.

    Required Repeatable
    no yes

    Note: Give it for a mesh that mesh-type, grid-spacing and domain › domain-size do not fix, e.g. an unstructured mesh of an irregular geometry. Its named boundaries are those that domain › boundary-condition › boundary refers to.

    Note: Give one entry for each file, e.g. the mesh structure and its boundaries when they are stored separately.

    Recommended: Base64. Other values are allowed.

    Note: Without encoding, contents is the file's text. With Base64, contents is the file's bytes encoded as Base64 (RFC 4648); use it for a binary file, or for a text file whose exact bytes matter.

    link

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    checksum*

    Required Repeatable
    yes yes
    algorithm*

    The algorithm that computed the checksum.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: SHA1, SHA224, SHA256, SHA384, SHA512, SHA3-256, SHA3-384, SHA3-512, BLAKE2b-256, BLAKE2b-384, BLAKE2b-512, BLAKE3, MD2, MD4, MD5, MD6, ADLER32. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications: The algorithm names of SPDX 2.3.1 §8.4 (File checksum field).

    value*

    The checksum of the file, in lowercase hexadecimal digits.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    yes no string [] inapplicable —

    Pattern: [0-9a-f]+.

    temporal-discretization

    The algorithm, step-size approach, and integration order used to advance a simulation, governing equation in time.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Forward Euler, Backward Euler, Adams-Bashforth, Crank-Nicolson, Runge-Kutta, IMEX. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Forward Euler" – First-order explicit: the rate is evaluated at the start of the step
    • "Backward Euler" – First-order implicit: the rate is evaluated at the end of the step
    • "Adams-Bashforth" – An explicit multistep scheme: the rate is extrapolated from previous steps
    • "Crank-Nicolson" – Second-order implicit: the rate is averaged over the start and the end of the step
    • "Runge-Kutta" – A multistage scheme: the rate is evaluated at intermediate points of the step, explicitly or implicitly
    • "IMEX" – Implicit-explicit: stiff terms are treated implicitly and the rest explicitly, e.g. a semi-implicit spectral scheme

    Note: Omit it for a stationary problem, which is solved without time integration.

    temporal-accuracy-order

    The exponent of the time step parameter (Δt) determining the rate at which the discretization error approaches zero as the time step tends to zero.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no int [] dimensionless —

    Minimum: 1.

    description

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no no string [] inapplicable —
    solver

    A numerical algorithm used to solve the discretized equations.

    Required Repeatable Type Extent Kind Rec. unit
    no yes string [] inapplicable —

    Recommended: Explicit, Direct, Conjugate gradient, BiCGSTAB, GMRES, Multigrid, Newton, Newton-Krylov, Picard. Other values are allowed.

    Clarifications:

    • "Explicit" – No system of equations is solved; the update is computed directly
    • "Direct" – A linear system is solved by factorization, e.g. LU or Cholesky
    • "Conjugate gradient" – A Krylov method for symmetric positive-definite linear systems
    • "BiCGSTAB" – Biconjugate gradient stabilized, a Krylov method for nonsymmetric linear systems
    • "GMRES" – Generalized minimal residual, a Krylov method for nonsymmetric linear systems
    • "Multigrid" – A linear solver or preconditioner that corrects the error on a hierarchy of coarser grids
    • "Newton" – Newton's method for a nonlinear system, each iteration solving a linear system
    • "Newton-Krylov" – Newton's method whose linear systems are solved by a Krylov method, often without forming the Jacobian
    • "Picard" – Fixed-point iteration of a nonlinear system, the nonlinear terms taken from the previous iterate

    Note: Give one entry for each layer of the solution, e.g. Newton, GMRES, and Multigrid as its preconditioner. The library or code that provides a solver is given as the computation's software.
