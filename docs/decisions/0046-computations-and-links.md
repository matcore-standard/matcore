# 0046 — Computations on equal footing, linked by label; the particle interaction model section

Date: 2026-11-09. Status: accepted. Decided by: Ellad Tadmor.

## Context
A comparison of the minimal and dft sections (2026-11-08) found that a dft section cannot say which computation it describes. Minimal `computation` is repeatable (e.g. a PBE relaxation followed by an HSE06 single point), while the dft section is one flat set of properties. Other sections have the same gap, and each links one computation to another in its own way:
- minimal `computation › interaction-model` (0028) names the kind of model that supplies an MD run's energies and forces, but not which computation or model;
- md `particle-interactions` describes the same model in more detail (model-type, bonding-type, theory-level, source), repeating part of it;
- mbpt `starting-point` names the DFT functional of the ground state a GW calculation starts from;
- ml `training-data › source` gives where training data came from;
- derived properties are "computed from data generated using a CMS method documented elsewhere".

The decider asked for computations used to generate results and computations used as interaction models to stand on equal footing, each a `computation` block with a required label, so that blocks can refer to each other and to labelled structures. A DFT block may be added to a record after the record is created, so the label it refers to must already be there.

Points settled in the discussion:
- **The role is a property of the link, not of the computation.** The same DFT computation can supply an MD run's forces and also be part of the published data; an MLIP is the result of a training computation and the interaction model of an MD run.
- **A computation is a group of calculations, never a single job.** Otherwise a dataset of thousands of single points needs thousands of blocks.
- **MatCore describes a dataset; it does not reproduce its execution.** Workflow managers (e.g. AiiDA, pyiron) keep a node per job, with order, control flow and complete inputs. MatCore keeps a few nodes per dataset and links only what a reader needs to interpret the data.
- **The interaction model's own properties belong to a method section, not to minimal**, since they apply to particle models only. md's `particle-style`, `particle-interactions`, `charge-interactions` and `magnetic-interactions` describe the model, not the dynamics.
- **Where a particle interaction model ends.** eFF (the electron force field) treats electrons as particles and is a particle model; tight binding solves for electrons and is not. A model that predicts a charge density or band structure but supplies no energies and forces is a property predictor, not an interaction model.

Alternatives weighed and rejected:
- **(a) Remove `interaction-model` from minimal and keep the model in md.** A record without md would not say what supplied the forces, and MC, which has no section, would lose it.
- **(b) Keep `interaction-model` in minimal as a category and the detail in md.** It keeps two mechanisms, and does not say which computation or model supplied the forces.
- **The intent (results or interaction model) on the computation, in `task` or `method`.** It forces a wrong choice when one computation plays both parts.
- **One minimal method term per kind of particle model (IP, MLIP, FF, CG).** The kinds differ on three axes, two of which (`bonding-type`, `particle-style`) the section already records; the term would repeat them.
- **An `in-dataset` flag on each computation**, saying whether its outputs are published. It serves only the case of a force engine whose outputs are not published; `description` and the files can say so.

## Decision
1. **`structure` gains a required `label`**, its first member: string, `[]`, inapplicable. Description: "A name for this structure, by which computations in the record refer to it." Pattern: the property-name pattern `^[a-z][a-z0-9]*(-[a-z0-9]+)*$`. Note: "Note: A label is unique among all labels of structures and computations in the record."
2. **`computation`** gets a new description: "A group of calculations in the dataset that share one method and one set of settings, e.g. the DFT single points of a training set." It gains, in this order before `method-class`:
   - **`label`**: required; as in item 1. Description: "A name for this computation, by which other computations and the method-specific sections refer to it." The same note on uniqueness.
   
   and after `method`:
   - **`task`**: required; string, `[]`, inapplicable. Open terms: Single point, Optimization, Dynamics, Sampling, Free energy calculation, Training, Property calculation. Description: "What the calculations of this computation do." Note defining each term: "Single point" – energies, forces or other quantities evaluated for given configurations, without moving them, including the evaluations another computation requests of an interaction model; "Optimization" – configurations or cells relaxed toward a minimum of the energy; "Dynamics" – configurations evolved in time; "Sampling" – configurations sampled from a distribution without a time evolution, e.g. Monte Carlo; "Free energy calculation" – a free energy, or a potential of mean force, computed from sampled configurations; "Training" – the parameters of a model fitted to data; "Property calculation" – a property obtained from given configurations or from the results of another computation, e.g. phonons, spectra, or a prediction by a trained model.
   - **`structure`**: optional, repeatable; string, `[]`, inapplicable. Description: "The label of a structure modeled by this computation." Note: "Note: Omit it for a computation that acts only on configurations supplied by a computation that uses it, e.g. the DFT that supplies the energies and forces of an ab initio MD run."
   - **`input`**: optional, repeatable; a container. Description: "Another computation in the record whose results this computation uses." Members, in order:
     - `computation*`: string, `[]`, inapplicable. Description: "The label of that computation."
     - `role*`: string, `[]`, inapplicable. Open terms: Interaction model, Initial configuration, Starting point, Training data, Parameters, Source data. Description: "What this computation takes from that computation." Note defining each term: "Interaction model" – the energies and forces of the configurations this computation evaluates; "Initial configuration" – the configurations this computation starts from; "Starting point" – an electronic ground state, density or wavefunction this computation starts from, e.g. the DFT ground state of a GW calculation; "Training data" – data a model is fitted to; "Parameters" – values used as parameters of this computation's model, e.g. a free energy or elastic constants in a phase field model; "Source data" – results from which this computation obtains a property.
     
     Note on `input`: "Note: Give an input only where the data of this computation cannot be understood without it. A record describes the dataset; it does not record the order of the calculations or how to repeat them."
3. **`simulation-conditions` is renamed `conditions`** and becomes optional. Its members and notes are unchanged. Its description gains: "Omitted when no condition is imposed, e.g. for a single point or a training computation."
4. **`interaction-model` is removed** (reverses 0028 item 3). `method`'s note becomes: "Note: Give the method of the calculations in this computation. When they use energies and forces from another method, describe that method as a computation of its own and name it in `input` with the role Interaction model."
5. **`method`** gains terms: TB under Electronic; MS under Atomistic; PIM under Atomistic and under Mesoscopic. The note adds: "TB" – Tight Binding; "MS" – Molecular Statics: configurations or cells of particles relaxed toward a minimum of the energy, using the energies and forces of an interaction model; "PIM" – Particle Interaction Model: energies and forces of a system of particles given as an explicit function of the particles' coordinates, which may include electrons treated as particles, without solving for a wavefunction or an electron density, e.g. an interatomic potential, a machine-learned interatomic potential, a bonded force field, a coarse-grained model or the electron force field.
6. **`software › role`** gains the term "Interaction model" – the implementation of a particle interaction model, e.g. a KIM model or a potential file. The model's identity is given in that software entry's `identifier` and `link`.
7. **Every method-specific section** (dft, pim, md, mbpt, ml, pf) holds its properties inside one required, repeatable top-level container `settings`. Description: "Settings shared by the computations named in it." Its first member is `computation*`: repeatable; string, `[]`, inapplicable; description "The label of a computation these settings apply to." A settings block names only computations whose `method` is the section's (dft: DFT; pim: PIM; md: MD and MS; mbpt: MBPT; ml: ML; pf: PF). The section's present top-level properties follow `computation`, unchanged. A record may omit any method section; a computation need not be named by any settings block.
8. **A new section, Particle Interaction Model (PIM) Metadata, key `pim`**, describes computations whose method is PIM. Its `settings` holds, after `computation`:
   - `theory-level*`: open terms Physics-based, Machine-learned (md `particle-interactions › theory-level` reduced to the origin of the functional form; its Tight-binding is the method TB, its Ab initio an Electronic computation named as Interaction model, its Ab initio machine learning a Machine-learned PIM, or an Electronic method when electrons are solved for);
   - `model-type*` (from md `particle-interactions › model-type`);
   - `particle-style*`, now repeatable, so a model with two kinds of particle (e.g. nuclei and classical electrons in eFF) lists both (from md `particle-style`);
   - `bonding-type*` (from md `particle-interactions › bonding-type`);
   - `charge-interactions` and `magnetic-interactions` (from md, unchanged), whole.

   A PIM computation is the evaluation of energies and forces (task Single point when another computation uses it), whatever code performs it, and its `software` lists every layer that takes part, e.g. LAMMPS as Simulation code and a KIM model as Interaction model (0043). `charge-interactions` and `magnetic-interactions` describe the interaction as evaluated, including the charge equilibration and the long-range summation the simulation code performs. Degrees of freedom propagated in time rather than solved for at each step (e.g. extended-Lagrangian charges, Drude particles, spins under LLG dynamics) are named in pim; their thermostats are md `thermodynamic-constraint` entries whose `system` names them. (Amended 2026-11-09: these properties couple the model and the simulation code, e.g. the potential supplies the parameters and form of charge equilibration and LAMMPS solves it, so they are not split between pim and md field by field, nor is the Coulomb term made a computation of its own.)
   
   md `particle-interactions › source` is given as the PIM computation's software `identifier` and `link` (item 6).
9. **md** describes molecular dynamics and molecular statics computations (method MD or MS); its introduction already includes energy minimization. It keeps, inside `settings`: `algorithm`, `computation-parameter` and `initialization` (from md `computation`) and `thermodynamic-constraint`. md `computation › mode` is replaced by minimal `task` and `method` (its Minimization is method MS with task Optimization); `algorithm` keeps the baseline's terms for minimization (e.g. FIRE, conjugate gradients) beside those for dynamics; `particle-style`, `particle-interactions`, `charge-interactions` and `magnetic-interactions` move to pim (item 8). 0042 item 3 still applies.
10. **mbpt `starting-point` is removed.** An mbpt computation names the DFT computation it starts from in `input` with the role Starting point; that computation's dft settings give the functional.
11. **§1.2**, the paragraph on combined methods, becomes: "Some methods supply energies and forces (e.g. DFT, or an interatomic potential), others use them to sample or evolve a system (e.g. MD). A dataset is described as one or more computations, each a group of calculations that share a method and its settings. A computation that uses the results of another names it, with the role the other plays: an ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) computation names the DFT computation that supplies its energies and forces. Each method-specific section describes the computations of its method. MatCore describes a dataset; it does not record how to reproduce its execution. The order of the calculations, their control flow and complete inputs belong to the provenance record of a workflow manager, to which a MatCore record may point."

Worked case: AIMD of liquid Si is a computation `aimd` (Atomistic, MD, Dynamics; structure `liquid-si`; conditions with the temperature; input `dft-forces` as Interaction model) and a computation `dft-forces` (Electronic, DFT, Single point; no structure, no conditions). A dft settings block names `dft-forces`; an md settings block names `aimd`. Classical MD replaces `dft-forces` by a PIM computation, named by a pim settings block, its software giving the potential's KIM ID or DOI. Relaxing a structure with an interatomic potential is a computation of method MS and task Optimization, named by an md settings block that gives the minimization algorithm, with the PIM computation as its Interaction model.

## Consequences
- One pull request applies items 1–7 and 11 to minimal, dft, the Preamble and the examples: examples/minimal.json labels its two structures and its computation and gives a task; examples/dft.json wraps its properties in `settings` naming that computation. It breaks 0.6.0 instances: labels and task are required, simulation-conditions is renamed, interaction-model is removed, and dft's properties move into `settings`. The check that every label is unique and that every reference names a label of the right kind is made by a test over the examples and by the step 9 instance validator.
- Items 8 and 9 are applied in the md pull requests of status step 5, which create pim; item 10 and the `settings` container of mbpt, ml and pf in those sections' pull requests. The ml pull request decides what remains of `training-data` once its source is an input with the role Training data; the derived pull request decides how a derived property names its source computation.
- PROPOSED, NOT DECIDED:
  - where pim is numbered among the sections. (Settled 2026-11-09: 2.3, before md, which it serves; md, mbpt, ml, pf and derived become 2.4–2.8 in their titles and menu labels. The existing pages keep their file names, so their addresses do not change; pim's page is docs/standard/pim.md, since the numbers in file names no longer follow the section numbers.)
  - whether a series of runs differing only in an imposed condition (e.g. one MD run at each of several temperatures) is one computation or several;
  - a citation for a model that comes from a paper (md `source › reference`), which software has no field for;
  - for QM/MM, which region each interaction model covers;
  - a minimal term for an Electronic method with a learned ingredient (e.g. a learned Hamiltonian);
  - a particle-style term for eFF's nuclei (e.g. Nucleus), since "Atom" means an individual atom;
  - KIM IDs as an Annex I scheme (already raised by 0043);
  - whether a DFT relaxation is one computation (DFT, task Optimization), as now, or an MS computation with the DFT computation as its Interaction model, as for AIMD; the second moves dft's relaxation tolerances to md (finding A4 of the minimal–dft analysis). (Settled 2026-11-09: one computation, method DFT and task Optimization; dft keeps its relaxation tolerances. MS is for relaxations driven with a particle interaction model.)
  - whether md's title names molecular statics. (Settled 2026-11-09: it does not; the title stays "Molecular Dynamics (MD) Metadata", whose introduction already includes energy minimization, and the key stays md.)
