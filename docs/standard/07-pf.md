# 2.7 Phase Field (PF) Metadata

<p class="mc-version" data-version="0.6.0">Version 0.6.0</p>

The metadata for Phase Field (PF) data (MatCore-PF) is tabulated below. This metadata documents key aspects of the PF method to provide understanding of the dataset generated by it. This includes the fundamental mathematical and physical framework governing the PF simulation, including the governing equations and thermodynamic free energy models. The properties are given in settings blocks, each naming the PF computations it describes. (Note: This standard inherits the metadata in the minimal MatCore standard, adding the properties below.)

<summary><span class="mc-name">physical-phenomenon<span class="mc-star" title="required" aria-label="required">*</span></span><span class="mc-icon mc-i-rep" title="repeatable: may appear more than once" aria-label="repeatable: may appear more than once"><svg width="18" height="18" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path d="M12 6v3l4-4-4-4v3a8 8 0 0 0-8 8c0 1.57.46 3.03 1.24 4.26L6.7 14.8A5.9 5.9 0 0 1 6 12a6 6 0 0 1 6-6m6.76 1.74L17.3 9.2c.44.84.7 1.8.7 2.8a6 6 0 0 1-6 6v-3l-4 4 4 4v-3a8 8 0 0 0 8-8c0-1.57-.46-3.03-1.24-4.26"/></svg></span></summary>

Material process being modeled; description of the overarching processes and mechanisms being modeled/simulated.

| Required | Repeatable | Type | Extent | Kind | Rec. unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yes | yes | string | [] | inapplicable | — |

Recommended: `Isothermal solidification`, `Directional solidification`, `Isothermal annealing`, `Sintering`, `Twinning`, `Grain growth`, `Spinodal decomposition`, `Fracture`, `Plasticity`. Other values are allowed.

**Clarifications:** "Isothermal solidification" – Growth of a solid from a melt or solution held at one uniform temperature, e.g. dendrites growing into an undercooled melt. "Directional solidification" – Solidification in an imposed temperature gradient, the front advancing along it, e.g. cellular or dendritic growth in a pulled sample. "Isothermal annealing" – Evolution of a solid microstructure held at one temperature, e.g. recovery, recrystallization or precipitate coarsening. "Sintering" – Bonding and densification of particles by diffusion below their melting point. "Twinning" – Formation or growth of twins, domains whose lattice is the mirror image of the parent's across a twin plane. "Grain growth" – Coarsening of a polycrystal by grain-boundary migration, driven by the reduction of grain-boundary energy. "Spinodal decomposition" – Phase separation of an unstable solution by the growth of composition fluctuations, without a nucleation barrier. "Fracture" – Initiation and propagation of cracks, a phase field marking the broken material. "Plasticity" – Irreversible deformation, e.g. by dislocation motion, described by phase fields or coupled to the phase field model.

Note: Give one entry for each process modeled.

<summary><span class="mc-name">type<span class="mc-star" title="required" aria-label="required">*</span></span><span class="mc-icon mc-i-rep" title="repeatable: may appear more than once" aria-label="repeatable: may appear more than once"><svg width="18" height="18" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path d="M12 6v3l4-4-4-4v3a8 8 0 0 0-8 8c0 1.57.46 3.03 1.24 4.26L6.7 14.8A5.9 5.9 0 0 1 6 12a6 6 0 0 1 6-6m6.76 1.74L17.3 9.2c.44.84.7 1.8.7 2.8a6 6 0 0 1-6 6v-3l-4 4 4 4v-3a8 8 0 0 0 8-8c0-1.57-.46-3.03-1.24-4.26"/></svg></span></summary>

The mathematical classification of the phase field equation.

| Required | Repeatable | Type | Extent | Kind | Rec. unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yes | yes | string | [] | inapplicable | — |

Recommended: `CH`, `AC`, `GP`, `FID`, `PFC`, `Navier-Stokes`, `Multi-phase-field`, `Mechanical equilibrium`, `Poisson`. Other values are allowed.

**Clarifications:** "CH" – Cahn-Hilliard: a conserved field evolves by a flux driven by the gradient of the variational derivative of the free energy. "AC" – Allen-Cahn: a nonconserved field relaxes at a rate proportional to the variational derivative of the free energy. "GP" – Grand potential: the phase fields evolve with the grand potential, the chemical potentials being the diffusion variables instead of the concentrations. "FID" – Finite interface dissipation: each phase has its own concentration within the interface, and the phases exchange solute at a finite rate. "PFC" – Phase field crystal: a conserved field periodic on the atomic scale resolves the crystal lattice and its defects. "Navier-Stokes" – The flow of a fluid phase; the evolved variable is the velocity. "Multi-phase-field" – Several phase fields, one for each phase or grain, constrained to sum to one. "Mechanical equilibrium" – The balance of stresses; the evolved variable is the displacement. "Poisson" – Electrostatics; the evolved variable is the electric potential.

Note: Give one entry for each classification that applies, e.g. AC and Multi-phase-field. Give an anti-trapping current, or a sharp- or thin-interface formulation, as a user-specified value.
